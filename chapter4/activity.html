Warm-up · Measures of variability

The 30-Second Challenge: see variance as squares

Each dot is one person's guess of 30 seconds. Each square has a side equal to that guess's distance from the mean. Variance is the size of the average square, in seconds². Standard deviation is the side of that average square, so it is back in plain seconds.

Deviation
How far one value is from the mean: x − x̄. Positive and negative deviations always add to zero, so we cannot simply average them.
Variance (s²)
The average squared deviation. Squaring removes the minus signs and punishes big misses. Its unit is squared (seconds², RM²), which is why it is hard to interpret on its own. Excel: =VAR.S()
Standard deviation (s)
The square root of variance. Read it as the typical distance of a value from the mean, in the original unit. Excel: =STDEV.S()
Coefficient of variation
SD ÷ mean × 100%. Use it to compare spread when the means or units differ.

s² uses n − 1 because our data is a sample. σ² and σ (divide by n) are for a whole population: VAR.P, STDEV.P.
